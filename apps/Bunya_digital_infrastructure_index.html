DNS & Mail Identity Guide

The records that connect Bunya services to the public internet.

These are explanatory cards only. Real values must come from the actual hosting, DNS, mail, verification, security, or registrar service.

A

IPv4 Website Address

Points a hostname to an IPv4 destination supplied by the hosting service.

AAAA

IPv6 Website Address

Points a hostname to an IPv6 destination where supported.

CNAME

Alias

Connects a subdomain to another hostname, often for hosted websites or applications.

MX

Mail Delivery

Directs incoming email toward the authorized E=Ven Mail or external mail servers.

TXT

Verification

Stores ownership checks, service verification, SPF policies, and other text values.

SPF

Authorized Senders

Declares which systems may send email on behalf of a domain.

DKIM

Signed Mail

Publishes a public key used to validate cryptographic signatures on outgoing mail.

DMARC

Mail Policy

Defines how receiving systems handle failed identity checks and where reports are sent.

Bunya Stewardship Principles

Infrastructure should be understandable, portable, and owned responsibly.

Members Keep Ownership

Domain ownership, renewal responsibility, transfer rights, billing, and administrative authority must be documented clearly.

No Hidden Credentials

Registrar tokens, DNS keys, DKIM private keys, mailbox credentials, and administrator secrets never belong in public HTML.

Honest Service Boundaries

A planning interface is not represented as a registrar, nameserver, email host, cloud provider, or deployment service until those systems exist.

Portability by Design

Members should be able to export plans, move domains, recover data, and change providers without losing access to their identity.

Security Before Scale

Authentication, permissions, logging, backups, recovery, monitoring, and incident response come before public provisioning.

One Network, Clear Roles

Bunya, E=Ven Mail, 333 Identity, HOLLO, KANSEE, and public websites should share identity while preserving separate permissions.

Development notice: Bunya is currently a public gateway and local infrastructure-planning interface. It does not yet operate authoritative DNS nameservers, register domain names, provision real mailboxes, host private files, process payments, or deploy server-side applications. Those functions require authenticated backend services, provider APIs, databases, monitoring, backups, billing controls, and legal ownership records.
